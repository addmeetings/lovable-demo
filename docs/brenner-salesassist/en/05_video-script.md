# 5. Video script – "A follow-up email in 3 minutes"

> **Translation for review.** The video itself is recorded in **German** (assignment requirement and audience). The German speaking text is in `../05_video-drehbuch.md`.

**Format:** screen and voice (Loom), German · **Length:** max. 7:00 min (target 6:30) · **Audience:** Brenner sales team, no AI experience
**Tone:** like in the workshop, formal "Sie" but relaxed, no jargon. Say "instruction" instead of "prompt", "AI" instead of "LLM".
**Preparation:** assistant open, the three test cases in a note ready to copy, an Outlook window with an empty email, notifications off.

> Note: The demo shows the **real** assistant. Outputs are generated live, not prepared. If an output differs, comment on it openly. That reinforces the message "always check".

| Time | Screen | Speaking text (approx. 130 words/min) |
|---|---|---|
| **0:00–0:35** Opening | Camera or title slide "Brenner SalesAssist" | "Hello everyone. Does this sound familiar? The quote is out, the customer doesn't get back to you, and you really should follow up. But between the building site, the phone and the next appointment, the email gets left – or it gets written in a rush. Ms. Brandt told me exactly that. In the next six minutes I'll show you how to get a follow-up email done in under three minutes, and in a way you can send with a clear conscience." |
| **0:35–1:20** What is it? | Claude project "Brenner SalesAssist" open (or Langdock) | "This is the Brenner SalesAssist. It's an assistant we built for exactly one job: follow-up emails on open quotes. You don't need any prior knowledge. You write a few bullet points, the way you'd tell a colleague, and you get a finished email back. One important thing up front: the assistant writes the draft, you decide. And it doesn't make anything up: no prices, no dates, no discounts." |
| **1:20–1:45** Getting started | Open a new chat in the project, type "Vorlage" → input template appears | "If you don't know what to enter, just type 'Vorlage'. It shows you a template: customer, quote, latest status, next step. That's all it needs." |
| **1:45–3:30** Demo 1: Standard | Paste T1, send, walk through the output, highlight with the cursor | "An example: Mr. Keller from Wohnbau Keller, a heat pump for an apartment building, he wanted to talk to his architect. I type that in as bullet points, just like that, and send it. … Let's look at what comes back. At the top is the subject with the quote number. Then a personal salutation, and it picks up the on-site visit and the architect. That makes the email personal. At the end there is exactly one clear request: a short call, Tuesday or Thursday. So the customer only has to say yes. And below that is the part you should never skip: 'Please check'. It lists what you still need to add. Then you copy the email into Outlook, read it through quickly and send it. That took just over a minute." |
| **3:30–3:55** Adjusting | Type "shorter" | "Too long? Just write 'shorter'. Or 'more formal', or 'second reminder'. You don't have to start over – you tell it what should be different, like you would a colleague." |
| **3:55–4:45** Demo 2: Incomplete | Paste T2, show the questions, then "with placeholders" | "Now everyday reality: you're in the car and quickly type 'follow-up email to Müller about heating'. What happens? It doesn't just start writing and guessing. It asks, but only for what is really missing. Here: Mr. or Ms. Müller? Company or private customer? If you're in a hurry, say 'with placeholders'. Then you get a draft with gaps in square brackets that you fill in later." |
| **4:45–5:45** Demo 3: Sensitive | Paste T3, show the output, highlight "Please check" | "And now a tricky case. The customer complained about a delay and mentioned a cheaper competitor's quote. Someone wants to offer ten percent off and add pressure: 'only until Friday'. Watch what it does. It takes her frustration seriously and offers a conversation. But it doesn't include the discount or the deadline. Down here it also explains why: the discount isn't approved, and the deadline is artificial. And right at the top it says: sensitive case, please check briefly with sales management. That's exactly how we want to treat customers." |
| **5:45–6:30** Three rules | Slide with 3 points | "Three rules to take away. First: facts in, email out. The more concrete your bullet points, the better the email. Second: always read before you send, especially the 'Please check' part. You send the email, not the AI. Third: don't enter sensitive data – no bank details, no health information. Name, company and quote are enough." |
| **6:30–6:55** Close | Slide: link to the assistant, contact person, workshop date | "My suggestion: try it this week for your next three follow-up emails. You'll find the link below the video. What works well and what doesn't, we'll collect in the workshop – that's exactly how we make the assistant better. Good luck!" |

**Total speaking text:** about 560 words in German, so roughly 4.5 minutes of speaking. Add about 1.5–2 minutes while the assistant responds and outputs are shown. Total about 6:30, leaving a buffer up to 7:00.
